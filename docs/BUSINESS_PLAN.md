# Hooray, a Telemachus company

### A plan to take Moonpig's market with the data it isn't using

Akshay Devon · 18 September 2026

---

## Summary

Moonpig makes £51.7m of profit after tax and converts 13% of the 113 million occasions it knows about. It sends a reminder. Nine times in ten, nothing happens.

Hooray drafts the card first and asks second. It sells to companies, where one signature brings hundreds of occasions and the cost is an operator's time, not an advertising budget. Then it reaches consumers through the people who work there.

**The pitch to a firm.** £30 per employee per year. Every person noticed on every occasion, nobody has to remember anything, and each employee gets six cards a year for their own family. At Moonpig's prices that's £8,500 of card value for £6,000, before the automation.

**The moat.** The list of people and dates. Everything else is replicable. Companies are the door because a signature is the cheapest way to put the consumer product in front of hundreds of people who'll build that list.

**The inefficiency is in the data.** Fifteen years of orders tell it who sends to whom, where they live, what they wrote and when. It uses that to send an email. It doesn't infer next year's occasion from this year's card, doesn't see that two customers with the same mother are siblings, doesn't convert the 36 million people who receive a card each year. Hooray does all of that from day one.

**This is not a UK-only plan.** Print is bought wherever the card lands, so a card to a mother in Mumbai is printed in Mumbai. The consumer product serves every country a print partner covers from the first day, and the company product enters the US, four times the UK with no online leader, through the same door.

**The aim is Moonpig's market.** A quarter of its UK revenue by year five is about 1.8 million consumers. The financials get to 300,000 by year three, seeded by employers in year one and grown by the product's own loops from year two, with the consumer line overtaking the company line in year four.

**Built today.** The whole loop, running: roster in, cards drafted, the weekly digest emailed through Brevo, the print request to Stannp on a key I got by phone this afternoon, collections that open themselves when someone leaves, gifts on milestones, a consent gate, a personal workspace.

**The ask.** £250k of capital over twelve months. One person to start, a second at day ninety, salaries carried by Telemachus. Three hundred accounts, 25,000 consumers, £2.55m of revenue, contribution-positive on operating costs by month nine. Gates at thirty, sixty and ninety days.

### The numbers

|---|---|

| Gross margin, Moonpig segment | 55.9% |
| Adjusted EBITDA | £104.6m, 28% |

| Free cash flow | £73.5m; about £65m returned to shareholders |

| Shipping and logistics | £88.2m, 23.6% of revenue, growing faster than sales |
| Customers, orders, order value | 12.3m, 36m, £9.32 |
| Reminders held | 113m |
| Employees | 676; 143 in operations |
| Revenue per employee | £552k |

Five times the Telemachus profit threshold. Half the revenue-per-employee bar. No serious challenger.

### Where it falls short

1. **87% of reminders produce nothing.** 9.2 held per customer, 2.92 orders, about 1.2 of them prompted. The chief executive: most customers use Moonpig for a small proportion of the occasions they celebrate. Their own figure is 18%.
2. **Marketing costs a customer's whole first year.** £38.7m against £39.5m of new-customer revenue. The base grew 2.8%.
3. **Growth is price, not demand.** Orders down 9.5% since FY22. Order value up 21%.
4. **Shipping is eating the margin.** Up 9.4% against revenue up 6.5%. Over 40% of card-only orders now pay for tracked delivery, because they were ordered late, because the reminder didn't work.
5. **Gifts are 39% of revenue on an 18% attach rate** that moves 0.2 points a year.

### What it does with its data

For 36 million orders a year Moonpig knows the sender, the recipient and their address, the relationship, the occasion, the design, the message inside, the gift, the price, and how many days early it came. Fifteen years of that is millions of real messages and a map of where 100 million people live and who loves them.

It sends a reminder email seven days out. It doesn't:

- Infer that a card to Sarah every May means May is Sarah's birthday
- Notice that two customers sending to the same "Mum" at the same address are siblings
- Do anything deliberate with the 36 million people who receive a card each year
- Draft your message from the twelve you've already written
- Stop reminding you about someone after you've sent a sympathy card

That's the inefficiency. Not the factory. The data.

### The 533 people

Only 143 of 676 staff work in operations, and the factory is lean. The rest: an engineering organisation capitalising £11.8m of software a year, a marketing function spending £38.7m, merchandisers for thousands of designs, customer service, and separate organisations for Greetz and the experiences business. Necessary for a catalogue-and-brand model. In ours, designs are generated, acquisition is a contract, and the editor is an email. Four functions survive: engineering, an operator, supply, compliance.

### The experiences business

Buyagift and Red Letter Days. A voucher agency reporting 93.8% gross margin because it books commission, not face value. Written down £56.7m in FY25, revenue down 4.5%. Not worth replicating; experience vouchers are already one option when a collection closes.

### Why it won, and why that advantage has ended

Founded June 2000. Printed from Guernsey, where cards under £15 shipped VAT-free until April 2012: twelve years of a 20% price edge while it built the brand and the list. Funky Pigeon was building its own Guernsey facility when the relief closed.

| What won it | Now |
|---|---|
| First mover | Category built |
| Twelve years VAT-free | Closed by law |
| Television | Costs £38.7m a year to hold |
| The list | Converts at 13% |
| Own factories, same-day dispatch | Only matters if the card is late |
| Bought Greetz | Needs £73.5m of cash flow |

The window shut in 2012. This year's annual report lists "agentic AI disintermediation" as a principal risk while the chief executive calls AI "an enabler within our model, rather than a structural change." Both can't be true.

## 2. Hooray

Moonpig sells at the reminder. We sell at the contract.

### For companies

Connect the HR system once. The calendar builds itself. Ten days before each occasion a card exists, in the firm's voice, signed by the right manager. One email a week: approve, change a line, or hold. Do nothing and it goes anyway at five days. The product demonstrates effort without anyone expending it.

Collections open on the occasions people give for: 78% for a leaver, 75% for a retirement, 22% for a routine birthday. Team-scoped, private contributions, recipient chooses the gift. The leaver collection exists nowhere else, because nobody can schedule a resignation ninety days out.

Milestones and client dates carry a company-paid gift, set by policy once. Quality is visible: the recipient sits three desks away.

### For people

Same loop, your own life. Import birthdays from Google or Microsoft contacts in one consent. Apple only allows contact-by-contact. Facebook closed friends' data to apps in 2018, so the import is from contact books, not social networks. What does work socially is a share link, "tell me your birthday": each person adds their own date, and every reply is a lead.

Ten days out, one email: Mum's birthday is in ten days, here's the card. Send, edit, or skip. Nothing goes without the tap unless you subscribe.

| Tier | Price | Covers |
|---|---|---|
| Free | £0 | Two cards a year; six through an employer |
| Circle | £29 a year | Eight cards, sent automatically |
| More | £3.99 a card | Beyond the plan, posted |

Annual, because occasions are annual. Moonpig Plus has 1.2 million subscribers at £10.99 a year, so people pay for a card subscription and the entry price should be low. Cash upfront, one decision a year.

What it does not do is same-day rescue. That remains Moonpig's strength.

### Why people want this

- UK office workers spend £1,715 a year on office occasions
- The average collection is £138 from twelve people
- 89% find organising one hard; 65% don't know how much to give; 72% would rather choose their own gift
- Firms pay £72 to £96 a head for perks platforms that only work if staff log in
- Moonpig for Business already has Salesforce and Gousto
- Print.one connects HR systems to printed cards in the Netherlands, with customers

We are not creating demand. People already spend, collect and subscribe, and do so inconsistently. We organise it, and the first ten pilots convert in month one.

## 3. The moat

The list. Print, design, drafting, the digest: anyone can build them. Nobody can build 113 million occasions quickly. This is a plan to build a list faster and cheaper than Moonpig did.

| Route | Yield | Cost per occasion |
|---|---|---|
| Paid consumer acquisition | 9 occasions per person | £5; doesn't pay back |
| Company roster | 200 work occasions per signature | An operator's time |
| Roster plus allowance | 200 people who now add their family | Same signature |
| Contact import | 10 to 20 per person | Nothing |
| Circle request | Every reply | Nothing, compounding |

Companies are the door because the signature pays the salaries and the allowance builds the list. Three consequences: the consumer product opens on day one; import and circle are in the first thirty days; the headline metric is personal occasions under management.

Scale: employers seed 18,000 consumers in year one; the loops take it to 25,000 and 225,000 occasions. By year three it's 300,000 consumers and four million occasions, 3.5% of Moonpig's list, converting better because the card is drafted, not reminded.

### How the list grows without data entry

Moonpig has the data for each of these. It does none.

1. **Every card creates the next occasion.** A birthday card makes next year's date. A wedding card makes an anniversary. A baby card makes a birthday for a person no contact book has. A sympathy card stops the reminders.
2. **The graph, not the list.** You and your brother both send to the same "Mum": the graph knows you're siblings and offers you his birthday, and one family card to her.
3. **The recipient is the next customer.** A code on every card: send one back, sender pre-filled. Thirty-six million cards a year reach a doormat, and Moonpig prints a logo on the back.
4. **Drafts learn from what you write.** By the fourth card to your mother it sounds like you. One office manager's edits teach the whole firm.
5. **Infer, don't ask.** Import, ask the circle, read last year's card. A list that's mostly inferred.
6. **Learn the real list.** Skip the same cousin three years and we stop drafting for the cousin.
7. **Attach where people spend.** A 40th, a 25th anniversary, a first Mother's Day. Not 18% flat.
8. **The address book.** Every reply is an address. Nobody else knows where people's mothers live.

Items one and three are cheap and go into the first ninety days.

## 4. The market

**UK cards: £1.51bn a year.** That's single-card retail sales. Moonpig's UK card revenue is about £203m of it, 13%, from 12.3 million customers. The other 87% is high street and supermarket, which is where the customer who forgot goes on the day. The category isn't growing on volume and doesn't need to.

**UK companies.** 38,435 firms with 50 to 249 staff employing 3.5 million people. £105m of seats if every one bought. With larger firms and client rosters, around £200m. Year one at 300 accounts is 0.8% of the core band.

**Relationship businesses.** Banks, wealth managers, brokers, property, partner-led firms. Each relationship manager holds 50 to 200 clients. A card on the client's birthday or deal anniversary, signed by the person they know, is what a good manager does when they remember. A bank with 500 managers is 50,000 contacts under one signature. Cards need no sign-off; gifts follow the hospitality policy, and every one is logged.

**International: our model travels. Moonpig's didn't.** In twenty-five years Moonpig built one meaningful market outside the UK, Greetz in the Netherlands at £51m, and it bought that. Its model needs a factory and a television brand in every country it enters. Ours needs neither. Print is bought from a local partner wherever the card lands, and acquisition is a roster, which reads the same in any language.

| Market | Size | Why it works |
|---|---|---|
| Netherlands | Greetz, £51m | Consumer demand proven; a Dutch HR-to-print product proves the company buyer |
| United States | $7.1bn, four times the UK | Hallmark and American Greetings hold 70% through shops. No online leader. Same door: firms |
| Ireland, Australia | Moonpig already ships to Australia via third parties | Demand exists; the print partners are there |

**The diaspora.** People have family everywhere. A card to a mother in Mumbai or a brother in Sydney is the occasion people most regret missing, and Moonpig posts it from Guernsey. We print it in Mumbai. The print-on-demand networks already have partners in more than thirty countries; a card to family abroad costs what one across town costs and arrives on the day. From the first day the consumer product serves every country a print partner covers, which makes the UK a launch market rather than the market.

## 5. Economics

**Price.** £30 per employee per year. Client rosters £0.50 per contact plus £3.60 a card, matching Moonpig's business price. We could survive a price war at 61% card margin; they can't go below their own consumer price.

**Cost.** £1.39 a card all in through Docmail, envelope and second-class post included. Stannp £1.15, no envelope, prototype only. I rang Stannp at four; they set up a free API trial on the call and the key was live by five. Moonpig's card costs less than ours by thirty to sixty pence. We don't compete on card cost. Print is £2.78 of a £30 seat. Card cost could double and the seat clears 70%.

**Supply.** Print bought, not built; a press is a year-three question. Gifts never held; Moonpig carries £8.7m of stock. Seats paid a year upfront, so no supplier terms needed; Moonpig runs on 134-day payables. Royal Mail rises hit both of us.

## 6. Go-to-market

### Start

UK professional services, 50 to 250 staff, with a CRM. London. Clean HR data, a client roster to expand into, a partner who currently writes the leaving card personally, and a £6,000 ticket under the procurement line.

Not firms over a thousand, under twenty, or in retail and hospitality. The exception is relationship businesses, where a bank or broker's size is the size of the prize. Quarter three, with references.

### The first fifty

The product sends cards. The first customers come from using it to send cards.

1. **The Telemachus network.** Its portfolio, its investors, its founders' contacts at firms of the right size. The first ten come from warm introductions, not from selling inside the building.
2. **A card, not an email, to the next hundred.** A hundred Heads of People at London firms of the right size. LinkedIn gives their start date. On their work anniversary, a real card lands on their desk, drafted by the system, signed by me, with one line on the back: your staff would get one of these on every occasion, and nobody at Hooray typed this. A hundred cards is £139. It's the demo, delivered.
3. **One free leaving collection.** The office manager is dreading next month's envelope. Run that one, no commitment. Eight colleagues sign, the leaver posts it, the Head of People sees the product at its best. Then ask about the roster.
4. **Where the champions already are.** Office managers and executive assistants have their own communities, thousands strong, all doing this by hand. One honest post.
5. **The open offer.** A public offer to run any firm's next leaving card free for a month. Every collection page reaches a whole team; every leaver shares it. The shared-card loop, triggered on purpose, at a pound a time.

| Roster | Buyer | Budget | The line |

| Clients | Managing Partner or Head of BD | Client entertainment | Every client, every milestone, without you remembering |

The champion is the office manager who does it by hand. Ask how, then ask who signs. Never pitch saved hours; £6,000 doesn't beat a hundred hours. Never say cheaper than Moonpig.

Moonpig spends £38.7m a year buying strangers. Every channel above reaches people the product has already touched, or costs nothing.

### The funnel

Twenty messages a day, 5% reply, half become pilots, 60% convert: one and a half accounts a week, seventy a year from cold outbound. The target is 300. The gap closes three ways: the Telemachus network and the hundred cards for the first thirty, shared-card inbound from month three, and the second person taking the product at day ninety so the first sells full time. Outbound alone is the floor; the three together are the plan.

Measure: reply rate, pilot rate, conversion, days to close, expansion at day 60, cost per account. Stop at £2,000 an account or six weeks a cycle.

### Sequence

Q1 London professional services, ten pilots to thirty paid. Q2 client rosters on every account; second city. Q3 multi-site groups and relationship businesses; partner channels. Q4 the third person on consumer growth; the paid-acquisition decision against a measured cohort.

### The endgame

Moonpig UK is £284.5m from customers spending £27 a year. From 300,000 at year three, that's two and a half times a year for two years: year five. The loops in section 3 deliver it. Short of that, this is a profitable business worth tens of millions. At it, every card we send is one Moonpig didn't, and its cost per new customer rises until the model breaks.
