# The questions you will be asked

Ten questions, the best answer, and where the answer is genuinely weak. Do not pretend the weak ones are strong. A trial day is judged on whether you think like an owner, and owners know where their plan is thin.

---

### 1. Why hasn't Moonpig already done this?

They have. Moonpig for Business launched in October 2025 at £3.60 a card, and it is a bulk shop: upload a spreadsheet, schedule up to ninety days ahead. No integration, no drafting, no collections. Automating it properly means connecting to HR systems, and their whole organisation is built around consumer acquisition.

The structural answer is channel conflict. They sell a card to a consumer for £5.89 all-in and to a business for £3.60. Making the business channel genuinely better teaches consumers to buy through the cheaper door and cannibalises their highest-margin line.

**Weak spot.** They could build it in six months if they decided to, or buy whoever wins. The honest answer is that the window is the eighteen months before they care, which is exactly the Telemachus model.

### 2. What is your moat?

None, and that is the thesis. Telemachus's own position is that AI dissolved moats. What the unit has is a cost structure the incumbent cannot match without dismantling itself, four people against six hundred and seventy-six, and speed.

The only compounding asset is the signed roster base and the occasion data that comes with it.

**Weak spot.** A well-funded copycat could do the same thing. The defence is being first to the relationships, which is an operator problem, not a technology one.

### 3. Is the market actually big enough?

This is the real weakness and you should raise it before they do. UK B2B occasion cards is worth tens of millions, not the £373m Moonpig makes.

The answer is three expansions in order. Staff rosters to client rosters, which is five to ten times the volume per account. Cards to collections and gifts, which is twenty to a hundred times the transaction value. Then employees who use a collection becoming consumer accounts at no acquisition cost, which is the only route into the consumer pool without an advertising budget.

**Weak spot.** The third expansion is unproven. Treat it as optionality, never as the plan.

### 4. Why would a company pay £30 per head for greeting cards?

Because it is not a stationery purchase. Reward Gateway charges £72 to £96 per employee per year and entry-level UK perks platforms start around £36. At £30 this is among the cheapest lines on a benefits schedule.

The sharper version: six cards cost us £8.34 and cost £35.34 at Moonpig's consumer counter. The employer pays £30 a head and the employee personally receives £35 of value, before the company's own occasions are counted. Moonpig cannot match that without undercutting its own consumer price.

**Weak spot.** No customer has paid anything yet. The price is benchmarked, not tested.

### 5. Who is your first customer and how do you get them?

UK professional services firms with fifty to five hundred staff and a CRM. Accountancy, financial advisers, law, recruitment, estate agency. Land on staff occasions where the buyer is people ops and the data is clean, then expand to the client roster where the volume and the willingness to pay are several times higher.

**Weak spot, and the one you must fill yourself.** Telemachus hires operators for the relationships and context AI cannot supply. If you can name one real firm and one real person you could call this week, say it. A named warm introduction is worth more in that room than another page of analysis.

### 6. Why is this a Telemachus unit rather than a startup?

It needs print supplier terms at volume, which is a relationship and a contract. It needs shared infrastructure for connectors, billing and compliance that would otherwise be rebuilt per unit. And it needs an operator with the standing to get in front of managing partners.

**Weak spot.** The account values are small and the motion is sales-heavy, which is the least Telemachus-shaped thing about it. Be ready to argue that client rosters and multi-site groups fix the average contract value, and that the consumer bridge is what makes the ceiling interesting.

### 7. What stops this becoming another gifting platform that dies?

Blueboard shut down in 2024. The enterprise gifting category is crowded and expensive. The difference is that gifting platforms sell a budget line that gets cut in a downturn, at fifteen thousand dollars a year and up.

This is a roster subscription at a benefits price point, where the cards go out whether or not anyone thinks about it. Cancelling means colleagues stop getting birthday cards, which is a visible internal decision rather than a quiet procurement one.

**Weak spot.** That is an assertion about churn, not evidence.

### 8. What did you actually prove today?

That the loop runs unattended on a simulated year, that the drafting is good enough to approve without editing, that the leaver collection works, and that the cost base is real because the print call goes to an actual provider.

Give the approve-without-edit rate as a number. It is the single metric that proves the AI layer is good enough to remove the human, which is the entire thesis.

**Weak spot.** One company, seeded data, no real customer and no real money.

### 9. If this fails in twelve months, why?

The approve-without-edit rate stays below sixty percent, so a human stays in the loop and the cost advantage evaporates. Or accounts stay at staff-only and never expand to client rosters, so the average contract value never clears the revenue-per-employee bar. Or Moonpig ships an HR integration and uses its brand to close the gap before the roster base is big enough to matter.

Having three specific failure modes with thresholds is the answer. Saying "execution risk" is not.

### 10. What would you do differently with the day?

Answer honestly. Something like: spent the first hour on the value chain rather than the code, because the reframe from consumer cards to company rosters was worth more than any feature. Or: should have built the collection flow before the dashboard, because it is the only thing no competitor has.

**Do not say "nothing."**
