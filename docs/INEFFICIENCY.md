# Every way Moonpig is inefficient

All figures from Moonpig Group plc FY26, year to 30 April 2026, unless marked. **[V]** verified from the annual report or results. **[D]** derived by arithmetic from verified figures. **[E]** estimate.

Telemachus looks for businesses making real money whose operations are "very far from the frontier of efficiency". This is the audit.

---

## 1. Demand generation: the big one

**They know about 113 million occasions and sell 36 million cards.**

| | |
|---|---|
| Occasion reminders held **[V]** | 113m |
| Orders placed in the year **[V]** | 36.0m |
| Share of orders placed within 7 days of a reminder **[V]** | ~40% |
| Orders actually caused by a reminder **[D]** | 14.4m |
| Reminder-to-order conversion **[D]** | 12.7% |
| Reminders that fire and produce nothing **[D]** | 98.6m, or 87% |

This is the single largest inefficiency in the business and everything else follows from it.

They have already won the hardest thing in commerce. The customer volunteered a list of the people they love and the dates that matter. Moonpig's response is to send an email saying "don't forget." Almost nine times in ten, the customer forgets anyway.

**Why it fails.** A reminder transfers work to the customer. It says: now go and choose a design, write something warm, type an address, and pay. That is ten minutes of effort triggered by an email arriving at a bad moment.

**What an AI-native version does.** It does the work first and asks for approval second. The card is drafted, addressed and ready. The customer taps yes. The conversion problem stops being a motivation problem and becomes a one-tap decision.

**Each customer tells them about 9 occasions and buys 2.9 times. [D]** 113m reminders across 12.3m active customers. The gap between what they know and what they sell is the business.

## 2. Customer acquisition: paying a full year's revenue for a customer

| | |
|---|---|
| Marketing spend **[V]** | £38.7m |
| Revenue from existing customers **[V]** | 89.4% |
| Revenue from new customers **[D]** | 10.6%, or £39.5m |
| Marketing spend against new-customer revenue **[D]** | roughly £1 spent per £1 earned |

Read that ratio carefully. Moonpig spends approximately the entire first-year revenue of a new customer to acquire them. The business only makes money from year two onwards.

Marketing also supports repeat purchase, so this is illustrative rather than a clean acquisition cost. **[E]** But the direction is not in doubt: £38.7m a year buys a base that is barely growing, up 2.8%.

| | |
|---|---|
| Marketing per order **[D]** | £1.07 |
| As a share of average order value of £9.32 **[D]** | 11.5% |

**What an AI-native version does.** Acquires through a business contract instead. One signature delivers a roster of hundreds of people with their occasions already structured, at no marketing cost.

## 3. Labour: 533 people who are not making anything

| | |
|---|---|
| Average employees **[V]** | 676 |
| Administration **[V]** | 533 |
| Operations, including the factory **[V]** | 143 |
| Revenue per employee **[D]** | £552k |
| Revenue per administrative employee **[D]** | £700k |
| Telemachus threshold | $1m, about £770k |

The factory is not the bloat. Only 143 people make and ship 36 million cards, which is genuinely efficient. The other 533 are product managers, engineers, designers, marketers, merchandisers, category teams, service and support.

Ask what those 533 people do. They maintain a catalogue of thousands of card designs, run licensing relationships, operate a personalisation editor, buy advertising, and answer questions about deliveries. In an AI-native business, the catalogue is generated, the editor is a prompt, the advertising is replaced by a contract, and the service is an agent.

## 4. The customer does the work, unpaid

Choosing a design, writing a message, uploading a photo, previewing and checking out takes roughly seven minutes. **[E]**

| | |
|---|---|
| Orders per year **[V]** | 36.0m |
| Customer time consumed **[D]** | ~4.2m hours a year |

This never appears on their income statement, which is exactly why it persists. It is the friction that produces the 87% reminder failure rate in section 1. The company treats customer effort as free and then pays £38.7m in advertising to overcome the resistance it creates.

## 5. Delivery: a cost that is growing faster than the business

| | FY26 **[V]** | FY25 **[V]** | Change **[D]** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | £373.0m | £350.1m | +6.5% |
| Shipping and logistics | £88.2m | £80.6m | +9.4% |
| Gross margin | 58.4% | 59.6% | down 1.2 points |

Shipping is 23.6% of revenue and rising faster than revenue. **[D]** Gross margin fell as a direct result.

The cause is revealing. More than 40% of UK card-only orders now choose tracked delivery. **[V]** Customers pay extra because they are anxious the card will not arrive in time. They are anxious because they ordered late. They ordered late because the reminder did not convert until the last moment.

**What an AI-native version does.** Drafts ten days ahead and dispatches five days ahead, so tracked delivery is never needed. That is roughly £1 of avoidable cost per order, on a £9.32 order.

## 6. Same-day printing solves a problem they created

Their proudest operational capability is printing and posting a card the same day. It is real and it is impressive.

It exists to serve panic buying. Panic buying exists because the reminder engine fails 87% of the time. The factory is an expensive cure for a demand-generation disease.

An occasion engine that works ten days ahead has no use for same-day anything. The incumbent's deepest operational moat becomes irrelevant rather than contested.

## 7. The catalogue is a liability pretending to be an asset

Thousands of designs, licensing agreements, merchandising teams, seasonal ranges, and the analytics to decide which cards to show. All of it exists to guess what the customer wants from a fixed inventory.

Moonpig used AI creative features in 31 million cards last year, more than double the year before. **[V]** They know generation works. But they are bolting it onto a catalogue business rather than replacing the catalogue with it, because the catalogue is where several hundred of those 533 jobs live.

## 8. Growth quality: price rises into a static base

| | FY26 **[V]** |
|---|---|
| Revenue | +6.5% |
| Orders | +2.1% |
| Active customers | +2.8% |
| Average order value | +5.7% |
| Orders per customer | 2.92, down from 2.94 |

Growth is coming almost entirely from charging more per order, not from more customers or more frequency. Frequency actually fell.

A business raising prices into a flat customer base with declining frequency has a finite runway. This is the most honest summary of Moonpig's position and it belongs in the plan.

## 9. Two of three segments are going nowhere

| Segment | FY26 revenue **[V]** | Growth **[V]** |
|---|---|---|
| Moonpig UK | £284.5m | +8.6% |
| Greetz, Netherlands | £51.0m | +1.5% constant currency |
| Experiences | £37.4m | -4.5% |

Moonpig UK carries the group. Greetz is flat. Experiences is shrinking, and was written down by £56.7m in FY25. **[V]**

Their one major acquisition destroyed value. That matters because it tells you what they will do with the £73.5m of free cash flow they generate: not build the thing that threatens them.

## 10. Capital allocation: they have run out of ideas

| | FY26 **[V]** |
|---|---|
| Free cash flow | £73.5m |
| Share buyback | £60.2m |
| Buyback planned for FY27 | up to £65m |

A company returning almost all of its free cash flow to shareholders is telling you it has nothing better to do with the money. For a challenger that is the most useful signal on the page. The incumbent has publicly committed its cash to buying its own shares rather than to defending its position.

## 11. The Plus subscription buys loyalty they should already have

| | **[V]** |
|---|---|
| Members | 1.2m, up 29.3% |
| Price | £10.99 a year |
| Benefit | 30% off every card, a free postcard monthly, first card free |
| Share of UK orders | 23% |

They are discounting a third off the product to secure repeat purchase from customers whose birthdays they already hold. The reminder data should deliver that loyalty for nothing. Paying for it is the clearest admission that the reminder engine does not work.

## 12. The business product is barely a product

Moonpig for Business launched in October 2025 at £3.60 a card including postage. **[V]** It offers spreadsheet upload and scheduling up to ninety days ahead. No API, no HR system integration, no drafting, no collections.

It does not appear anywhere in the annual report. **[V]** A product with named customers including Salesforce that is not worth mentioning to shareholders is sub-scale by definition.

## 13. The strategic trap: they cannot fix the business channel

| | **[V]** |
|---|---|
| Consumer price, card plus first class post | £5.89 |
| Business price, including postage | £3.60 |

The business channel is already 39% cheaper than the consumer one. Making it genuinely good, with integrations and automation, teaches consumers to buy through the cheaper door. Every improvement to their B2B product cannibalises their highest-margin line.

This is why the gap persists and why it will keep persisting. It is not an oversight. It is a structural constraint.

## 14. They have named the threat and misjudged it

The FY26 annual report adds a new principal risk called **"agentic AI disintermediation"**. **[V]** The stated defence is high-intent, bottom-of-funnel purchases and vertically integrated fulfilment.

In the same document the chief executive frames AI as "an enabler within our model, rather than a structural change." **[V]**

Those two positions cannot both be true. Either AI is a principal risk to the business model, or it is a feature you add to it. An incumbent that has written the threat down and then explained why it does not apply is the ideal target.
