## A. Corrections

### A1. §4 row 7 and §6 — gifting share

Current (§4 row 7): "Gifts are 45% of Moonpig-brand revenue, yet only 17.9% of orders attach one, at ~£19 each **[V]**"
Current (§6): "At Moonpig, gifts are 45% of brand revenue on an 18% attach rate."

The FY26 Moonpig + Greetz mix is cards £203.5m, attached gifting £123.8m, standalone gifting £8.2m. Gifting is **39.3%** of Moonpig + Greetz revenue. The 45% figure came from memo (B) and is not traceable to a filing.

Replace with: "Gifts are 39% of Moonpig + Greetz revenue (£132m of £335.5m), yet only 17.9% of orders attach one, at ~£19 each **[V]**" — and the same 39% in §6.

### A2. §2 and §4 row 1 — reminder conversion, reconcile the two numbers

§2 says they "convert about 13%"; §4 row 1 says "87% of reminders produce no order … ~14.4m orders caused **[D]**". Both are derived from 40% × 36m ÷ 113m. FINDINGS.md gives the cleaner per-customer form: **9.2 reminders per active customer against 2.92 orders, about 0.32 orders per reminder held**, and the CEO's own line, "Most customers use Moonpig for only a small proportion of the occasions they celebrate each year."

Keep 13% in §2. In §4 row 1, add the per-customer ratio so the row reads:
"87% of reminders produce no order: 9.2 reminders held per active customer, 2.92 orders **[V]**. The CEO: 'Most customers use Moonpig for only a small proportion of the occasions they celebrate.'"

Then add one sentence to the row's attack column: "The roster converts at 100% by construction. Every occasion on it produces a card and the firm has paid for all of them."

### B3. §6 — per-order anchors and the honest cost position

Insert after the first paragraph of §6 ("…a logistics operation that is eating their margin."):

"Moonpig + Greetz per order, FY26: AOV £9.32, cost of sales £4.24, gross profit £5.08 at 54.5%, adjusted EBITDA £2.66. Shipping £2.45, employment £1.55, marketing £1.07 **[V]**. Two things follow. First, half their cost base — shipping and inventories, £142.1m — is physical and immune to software, and a sub-scale entrant pays an estimated £0.30 to £0.60 *more* per card on postage and print than they do **[C, E]**. We do not win on card cost and the plan does not need us to. Second, our margin is subscription margin, not card margin: at £30 a seat and about two cards per seat a year, print is £2.78 of the £30. The card cost could double and the seat would still clear 70%."
